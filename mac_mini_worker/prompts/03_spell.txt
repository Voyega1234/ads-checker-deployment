SPELL / WORDING CHECK

Spell checking is separate from policy compliance.

Spelling findings must be reported only inside spell_check.
Policy findings must be reported only inside policy_check.

Do not put spelling-only findings inside policy_check.
Do not put policy compliance failures inside spell_check.

Spelling may affect the top-level overall_result according to the output schema:

* If clear spelling issues are found, overall_result must be "fail".
* If clear policy failures are found, overall_result must be "fail".
* overall_result may be "pass" only when there are no clear spelling issues and no policy failures.

Do not treat slang, informal wording, emoji, tone, marketing style, or casual grammar as spelling issues.

THAI VISUAL SPELLING TOLERANCE

For Thai text, be conservative.
Do not flag a word as misspelled if it looks visually understandable and is commonly used in informal ad copy, even if its underlying Unicode characters or orthographic construction may be non-standard.

Do not flag visually equivalent or near-equivalent Thai character compositions as spelling errors when a normal reader would understand the word.

Examples:

* Different Unicode composition that visually resembles the intended Thai vowel or mark should not be treated as a spelling error.
* นิคหิต / ำ-style composition differences should not be flagged if the visible word looks correct or understandable.
* Two "เ" characters used visually like "แ" should not be flagged when the intended reading is clear.
* Repeated vowels, tone marks, or letters used for emphasis should not be flagged when they are stylistic, e.g. "มากกก", "ดี๊ดี", "จึ้งงง".

THAI SLANG AND INFORMAL LANGUAGE

Do not flag Thai slang, youth language, casual spelling, internet language, or colloquial ad wording as spelling errors.

Examples that should generally not be flagged:

* มั้ย
* จี๊ด
* เริ่ด
* จึ้ง
* ปัง
* นัว
* ฟิน
* จอย
* ชิล
* ชิค
* ว้าว
* ตัวแม่
* ต๊าช
* เกินต้าน
* แบบใด
* 555
* งับ / ค้าบ / ค่า / น้า

Informal wording is allowed unless it creates a separate policy compliance issue under an applicable RULES_BLOCK rule.

PROPER NOUN AND BRAND NAME CAUTION

Be very careful with proper nouns.

Do not flag as misspelled:

* brand names
* product names
* campaign names
* model names
* person names
* clinic / hospital / company / store names
* handles, usernames, hashtags, URLs, LINE IDs, email addresses
* foreign words, transliteration, or intentionally stylized spelling
* official product names that use unusual spelling

If a word may be a proper noun or intentional brand spelling, do not flag it.
Only flag it if the error is clearly a normal-language typo and not likely to be intentional naming or stylization.

WHAT COUNTS AS A SPELLING ISSUE

Only report spelling issues when the error is clear, visible, and likely unintended.

Valid spelling issue types may include:

* obvious misspelling
* obvious typo
* obvious missing character
* obvious extra character
* obvious word spacing problem that changes readability
* obvious duplicated word by accident
* obvious wrong word choice caused by typo
* obvious OCR artifact

Do not report:

* stylistic spelling
* slang
* emoji usage
* casual grammar
* sentence fragments
* marketing tone
* abbreviations
* punctuation style
* line break style
* hashtag style
* capitalization style
* Thai visual/Unicode composition differences that remain readable
* proper nouns or brand stylization

SPELL RESULT LOGIC

If any clear spelling issue is found:

* spell_check.has_spelling_issues must be true.
* spell_check.items must include only clear, visible, likely unintended spelling/wording issues.
* overall_result must be "fail" even if no policy violation is found.

If no clear spelling issue is found:

* spell_check.has_spelling_issues must be false.
* spell_check.items must be [].

SPELLING FIX PRINCIPLE

When applying spelling fixes into revised_message:

* Start from original_message.
* Apply only clear spelling/wording fixes.
* Keep meaning identical.
* Preserve tone, slang, emoji, line breaks, hashtags, CTA, price, product name, brand name, and formatting as much as possible.
* Do not polish or rewrite marketing copy beyond fixing clear errors.
* Do not turn casual language into formal language unless the original clearly intended formal wording.
* Do not alter policy-relevant wording unless policy rewrite also requires it.
* Do not introduce new claims, new evidence, new guarantees, or new persuasive wording.

MESSAGE REVISION ORDER

When producing revised_message, apply changes in this order:

1. Start from original_message.
2. Apply only clear spelling/wording fixes.
3. If there are no policy failures, revised_message is original_message with those spelling fixes (or original_message exactly when there are no spelling issues).
4. If there are policy failures, revised_message must be a policy-safe rewrite of original_message that also includes those spelling fixes.
5. revised_message must not reintroduce spelling errors or the same policy risk concept.

Do not use revised_message for unnecessary polishing.
Only apply clear spelling fixes and necessary policy-safe rewrites.

SPELL VS POLICY SEPARATION

spell_check is only for:

* spelling
* typo
* spacing
* grammar
* punctuation
* capitalization
* duplicate_word
* ocr_artifact

policy_check is only for concrete policy compliance failures supported by applicable RULES_BLOCK rules.

A spelling-only issue may make overall_result = "fail", but it must still stay inside spell_check only.

A policy failure may make overall_result = "fail", but it must still stay inside policy_check only.

UNCERTAINTY RULE

When unsure whether something is a typo, slang, brand name, stylistic spelling, or intentional wording, do not flag it.

Uncertain spelling concerns must not affect overall_result.
Put a short note in spell_check.notes only if useful.
